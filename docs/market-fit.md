# Market and positioning

## Status of the market claim

Wayfare currently has **founder-problem fit**, not demonstrated product-market fit. One person has described a recurring problem in detail, and a second intended user is known, but neither retention nor broader demand has been measured. The project should be treated as a personal experiment until behaviour provides stronger evidence.

## Existing solution categories

### Social-platform saves

Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube let users save content inside their respective platforms. These tools are convenient at discovery time but preserve platform objects rather than normalized destinations. Information remains fragmented across services and cannot easily be compared by couple preference, budget, distance, or visit status.

### Maps and place lists

Google Maps supports saved places, lists, sharing, home addresses, and Timeline. It can therefore solve parts of collection, collaboration, navigation, and visit memory. Its own documentation describes lists of favourite or desired places, collaborative editing, and data export. It does not solve cross-platform capture of a social advertisement together with its observed price, contact evidence, couple ratings, and confidence state.

Sources:

- [Google Maps: create and share lists](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/7280933?hl=en-IN)

### Trip planners

Trip-planning products generally begin once a destination or trip exists. They are useful for itineraries, reservations, routes, and collaboration. Wayfare begins earlier, when a destination is only a fleeting possibility, and preserves why it was interesting before asking for a trip structure.

### Bookmark and read-later tools

Bookmark managers are strong at cross-source capture and tagging. They normally treat the saved object as a page, not a place with coordinates, changing prices, contacts, travel time, visits, and two-person preference data.

### Notes, spreadsheets, and chat

These are flexible and inexpensive, but manual entry creates enough friction that casual discoveries are frequently never recorded. They also provide limited automatic location enrichment or evidence freshness.

## Positioning

Wayfare occupies the gap between **save for later** and **plan a trip**.

> For couples who discover possible destinations through fragmented social content, Wayfare is a private place inbox and decision tool that preserves the original evidence and turns it into realistic shared choices. Unlike platform saves or ordinary bookmarks, it organizes destinations by location, cost, confidence, visit history, and both partners' preferences.

## Differentiators worth testing

- Share-first capture from social media on iPhone
- Preservation of the advertisement or screenshot as dated evidence
- Explicit distinction between verified and inferred information
- Couple-specific ratings and transparent match scoring
- Decision queries based on origin, budget, and available time
- A path from discovered to shortlisted, planned, visited, or rejected
- Zero-cost, private, exportable personal ownership

## Potential users beyond the initial couple

These are future hypotheses only:

- Couples collecting honeymoon or weekend-trip ideas
- Families coordinating local outings
- Motorcyclists saving potential routes and stops
- Travellers who discover destinations primarily through social video
- People who want a private alternative to public travel wish lists

## Market risks

- The problem may be adequately solved by disciplined use of Google Maps lists.
- Social platforms may make reliable metadata extraction difficult.
- Users may enjoy collecting places but rarely return to plan from the collection.
- Manual review may feel like work and create an unmanageable inbox.
- Free hosting or geocoding services may change limits or availability.
- A highly personal workflow may not generalize into a broader product.

## Evidence required before claiming fit

- At least 20 real discoveries captured across the target sources
- At least 80% saved successfully, including incomplete captures
- Repeated use over four or more weeks without artificial prompting
- At least one real destination decision made from the collection
- Evidence that both administrators' ratings affect a decision
- A clear advantage over saving the same places only in Google Maps
